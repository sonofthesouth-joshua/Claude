# Step 2 — Map the Friction

**What you're building:** the Friction Map — every automation candidate in your business, scored by ROI and ranked, with the math visible. This is the step most people skip, and it's why most AI projects automate the loudest annoyance instead of the highest-return bottleneck.

**Time:** 30–45 minutes of interview, then the agent scores and writes the map. One session.

## Before you start

- Step 1 must be done (the skill checks and will stop otherwise).
- Know your loaded hourly cost per role — what an hour of each role actually costs you (salary + overhead). Rough is fine; the agent marks estimates as assumptions and the math stays auditable.

## Run it

> Read and follow `000 OS/skills/bottleneck-finder/SKILL.md`.

The agent interviews you function by function. Two things to know:

- **It opens by asking what YOU want to automate.** Everything you name stays in the final list no matter what the data says — the ranking argues alongside your wishes, never behind your back.
- **The probes are the point.** "What does {person} actually do all day?" · "What breaks if {person} is sick two weeks?" · "What do you do just because it's always been done that way?" The best candidates usually come from these, not from your list. Answer them honestly.

When it proposes time estimates, correct them — you know your business. Every number ends up in the map with its source attached.

## Reading the map

`200 Notes/Company/Friction Map.md`:

- The **ranked table** — value, effort, payback, risk per candidate.
- The **contrarian callout** — where the data disagrees with what you planned. Read this twice. It's the most valuable paragraph in the kit.
- The **build queue** — the 2–3 candidates Step 3 should forge first.

Sign off on the queue (the agent will walk you through resolving every open flag first).

## Done when

The map exists, every number has a source or a written assumption, and you've signed the build queue.
